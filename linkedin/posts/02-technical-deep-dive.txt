Why XGBoost + Random Forest instead of a neural network for credit scoring?

Because a bank does not buy AUC. It buys a decision it can defend.

A neural net will fit the public default datasets. It will not give you a TreeExplainer that adds up, feature by feature, to the score on the page. SBP’s expectation for automated credit is explainable AI — adverse-action language, not a saliency heatmap a relationship manager cannot file.

So ForiFlow uses a soft-voting ensemble: XGBoost for the non-linear splits, Random Forest to keep a second opinion, probabilities averaged. SHAP runs on the trees. The officer sees a waterfall: facility-to-turnover +20, clean officer-entered repayment history +6, short operating life −3. Those numbers sum to the gauge.

I also put monotone constraints on the XGBoost member. A cleaner payment history is not allowed to increase predicted risk. That is a modelling choice you cannot audit inside a black box.

Interpretability is not a nice-to-have in this market. It is the licence to put the model in production.
